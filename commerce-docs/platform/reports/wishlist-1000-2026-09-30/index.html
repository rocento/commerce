What was tested

100 authored sentence patterns expanded into 1,000 unique phrases through product names and polite variations. These are synthetic regression examples used to improve the rules, not independent customer traffic or an unseen accuracy benchmark. Products span Grocery, Jewelry and unrelated names to check shared rules. The baseline was measured on the initial local implementation; the final corpus passed against both deployed language-engine endpoints (1,000 checks per store).

PASS means the expected parser contract was met. It does not mean 1,000 writes were executed. Product identity, sign-in, inventory, permission and server receipts remain necessary. Broad names such as “milk” can ask the shopper to choose an exact product. Ten explicit copy examples pass by returning unsupported guidance; copying while retaining saved items is not implemented in chat.

The release adds saved-item aliases, save-for-later and removal phrases, transfer paraphrases, verified bulk wishlist removal (up to 100 saved items), transfer quantity correction, exact-name handling and punctuation preservation for Jewelry product names, completion and chat-history serialization fixes, and the missing POST-only bulk-transfer proxy route. Validation includes 39 language regression tests, 148 backend tests and TypeScript checking. Live checks inspect actual wishlist/cart state on isolated demo accounts, including request replay and cleanup.
